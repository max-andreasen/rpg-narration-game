Ghouls were not always monsters. Ancient burial rites described them as watchers of the dead, bound by sacred magic to ensure that souls safely crossed into the afterworld. But when the great famine swept the desert kingdoms, the watchers were abandoned without offerings and slowly starved.

In desperation, they fed on the bodies they were meant to protect. The ritual magic twisted with their hunger, warping them into the pallid, bone-thin creatures known today. Now ghouls roam forgotten tombs and ruined cities, driven by echoes of their old purpose — endlessly searching for the souls they failed to guard, unable to distinguish the living from the dead.

Some travelers swear they’ve heard ghouls whisper apologies between their ravenous breaths, as if begging the dead for forgiveness.
