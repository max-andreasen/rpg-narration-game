Elder: 
The Elder Valerius is the administrative and spiritual anchor of the Starting Village. Formerly known as Valerius the Time-Tethered, he was once a high-ranking Chronomage who served in the distant capital during the Age of Ruin. He earned his title by casting the "Great Stasis," a massive ritual that sacrificed his own magical potency and physical youth to establish the village's permanent state of peace and its protective Verdant Shield.
He maintains the Ever-Ink Ledger, a massive, self-updating tome that records the name and arrival time of every soul that crosses the twelve basalt boundary stones. Valerius claims he no longer possesses magic, yet he is never seen sleeping, and his eyes are said to reflect the constellations of seasons that have not yet occurred. He resides in a house where the door is intentionally built to open inward, a symbolic gesture representing his philosophy that knowledge should be invited in and held close rather than projected outward.
Despite his frail health (50 HP), Valerius possesses a "Passive Resonance" that prevents any hostile action from being taken within his immediate presence. He provides new travelers with their initial permits and is the only resident capable of reading the ancient runes on the boundary stones. Local lore suggests that as long as Valerius remains within the village limits, the "Crying Gale" from the Rocky Hills will never reach the valley. He carries a staff made of Iron-Bark that never touches the ground, hovering a fraction of an inch above the soil to avoid "disturbing the earth's memory."

Blacksmith: 

Garrick the Blacksmith is the proprietor of the Soot-Stained Anvil, a workshop built into the side of a granite hill to naturally vent the heat of his forge. Despite his intimidating physical stature and high resilience (80 HP), he is known for a gentle temperament and a refusal to forge weapons of war, preferring instead to craft "tools for the living."
Garrick is the master of a rare metallurgical technique known as Hearth-Binding. By infusing molten iron with the charcoal of Iron-Bark oaks found in the nearby Enchanted Forest, he creates metal that is exceptionally durable and resistant to magical corrosion. He is the only villager who knows the exact path to the fire-resistant Iron-Bark groves, a secret he guards to prevent over-harvesting.
In the corner of his shop lives Ember, a pet magma-slug that Garrick rescued from a deep fissure in the Rocky Hills. He uses the creature's natural heat to perform intricate jewelry repairs and fine-tuning on delicate adventurers' gear. Garrick’s hands are so heavily calloused from years of working with enchanted metals that he can briefly handle red-hot coals without injury.
Locals believe his anvil is an ancient artifact itself, as it rings with a clear, melodic tone that can be heard across the valley, signaling the start of the workday. While his Evilness (0) is absolute, he is a formidable defender of the village; it is said that during a long-forgotten bandit raid, Garrick used a massive iron tongs to pluck arrows out of the air before they could reach the village children.

Fairy: 
The Fairy, known in sylvan tongue as Pip the Flicker, is a manifestation of the Enchanted Forest’s ambient mana. Unlike the stoic Sentinel Maples, Pip is a creature of high energy and low focus, possessing a faint Evilness (10) that stems from a lack of human morality rather than true malice. This "evilness" usually manifests as harmless tricks, such as knotting a traveler’s bootlaces together or mimicking the voice of a loved one to lead someone a few steps off the path.
Pip’s physical form is composed of Living Phosphor, allowing her to glow with an intensity that can pierce the forest's silver mist. She offers guidance to travelers, but her directions are often cryptic or delivered in the form of riddles that require the traveler to trade a "trivial thing"—like a button, a strand of hair, or a secret—to receive the true path.
Pip is particularly obsessed with the Mirror-Wing Starlings from the Starting Village and will often trade valuable forest secrets for a single blue feather from one of those birds. Despite her fragile Health (30), she is nearly impossible to catch; she can "Phase-Step" between the physical world and the ethereal plane, appearing only as a streak of light when threatened.
She resides in the Hollow of Echoes, a tree stump lined with "Memory-Moss" that records every secret Pip has ever overheard from the trees. It is said that Pip was originally a fragment of the Archmage’s ward that became self-aware, which is why she remains helpful to those seeking to navigate the woods, provided they can tolerate her mischievous nature.

Goblin: 

The Goblins of the Enchanted Forest belong to the Moss-Skin Marauders, a splintered clan of outcasts who have adapted to the forest's spatial instability. Their skin has a photosynthetic quality, allowing them to remain perfectly still and blend into the Sentinel Maples for days while waiting for an ambush. With an Evilness of 75, their motives are purely predatory; they view travelers as "walking larders" and loot-carriers.
They are immune to the forest's Slip-Fold phenomenon because they navigate using "scent-trails" of fermented Glow-Cap juice, which they smear on the trees to mark stable paths. Their primary weapons are Thorn-Darts coated in a paralytic resin harvested from Phase-Vines, designed to immobilize a target so they can strip them of their belongings without a struggle. Despite their relatively low Health (45), they are dangerous in groups due to their "Cackle-Sync," a high-pitched vocalization that mimics the forest’s own whispering to disorient victims.
Goblins are known to hoard "junk" they consider sacred, particularly rusted iron from the Starting Village, which they believe grants them protection against the forest’s spirits. They often set Trip-Root traps—mechanical snares made of braided vines and jagged obsidian shards—near the edges of the forest where the Merchant’s Crossroads begin. Local legend claims the Goblins are actually descendants of travelers who got lost in the woods centuries ago and were slowly warped by the forest's ambient magic and their own desperation. They have a particular hatred for Pip the Flicker, as the fairy often reveals their hiding spots to well-armed adventurers in exchange for shiny baubles.

Gigant spider: 

The Colossal Weaver is the apex predator of the Rocky Hills, a monstrous arachnid that has evolved to perfectly match its jagged, sun-bleached environment. Its carapace is not smooth but resembles cracked, grey limestone, allowing it to remain completely invisible while clinging to the ceilings of the Echo-Caves. With an Evilness of 90, it does not just hunt for hunger; it is known to "decorate" its lair with the polished belongings of its victims, displaying a cruel, trophies-based intelligence.
Standing on eight spindly, chitinous legs tipped with obsidian-hard points, the spider has a formidable Health of 120. It utilizes vibration-sensing hairs called trichobothria that are so sensitive they can detect a traveler's heartbeat through the solid rock floor from several chambers away. Its primary weapon is a Corrosive Silk that is woven into "Ghost-Webs"—nearly invisible strands that don't just trap prey but slowly dissolve armor and skin upon contact.
Despite its lethality, the Weaver is plagued by extreme photophobia. Because it has spent centuries in the light-swallowing darkness of the caves, its multiple eyes are hyper-sensitive. A steady lantern light causes the spider physical pain and confusion, disrupting its ability to triangulate vibrations. This forces the creature to retreat into the deeper "Veins of Silence" where light cannot reach.
Local legends say the Giant Spider was once a guardian of the mountain's secrets, but it was corrupted by the dark energies radiating from the Obsidian Tower. It is rumored that the spider’s venom, if harvested carefully, is the only substance capable of dissolving the Seal of Seven Sorrows at the tower's entrance, though few have survived long enough to test this theory.

Dark Wizard: 

The Dark Wizard, known as Malakor the Unmaking, is the architect of the Obsidian Tower and the source of the rot spreading through the surrounding lands. With an Evilness of 100, he is a being of pure malevolence who has long since discarded his humanity to become a living conduit for the Void. His physical form is a shifting silhouette of Animated Shadow, bound together by tattered robes of "Star-Slayed Silk" and held in place by a crown of jagged obsidian that is fused directly into his skull.
Malakor possesses a formidable Health of 200, sustained not by biology but by the Ever-Dying Flame at the tower's core. He practices a forbidden school of magic known as Atheogenesis, which allows him to unwrite the physical laws of his immediate surroundings—turning air into lead or light into physical blades. His primary weapon is the Staff of Sundered Souls, a focus made from the calcified remains of a titan's finger, capable of firing bolts of "Entropy-Fire" that ignore physical armor and strike directly at a target's life force.
His residence at the top of the Void’s Needle allows him to overlook the entire realm, and it is said that his gaze is what maintains the "Heavy Vapor" over the Misty Lake and the "Crying Gale" in the hills. Malakor does not sleep; he spends his centuries in the Chronos-Laboratory, attempting to perfect a ritual that will expand the tower's "Shadow-Choked Corridors" until they consume the Starting Village.
Despite his immense power, he is bound by the Law of Equal Loss—every time he uses his highest-tier spells, a small piece of his physical form dissolves into ash, which is why he constantly seeks to harvest memories and souls from travelers at the Seal of Seven Sorrows. Legend speaks of a time when Malakor was a student of the Starting Village’s Elder, Valerius, and that their rivalry is the reason the village is shielded while the rest of the world slowly fades into the dark.

Wandering Trader: 

Silas the Stout, the Wandering Trader, is a master of "Salesman’s Glamour," a low-level magical charisma that makes his eyes sparkle and his voice carry clearly over the din of the Merchant’s Crossroads. He operates the Gilded Mule, a massive, rickety cart pulled by a beast of burden that is never seen, as the harness leads into a swirling pocket of magical fog. His Evilness of 15 represents his "flexible" ethics; while he won't kill for gold, he has no qualms about overcharging desperate adventurers or omitting the fact that a "magic" ring only works on Tuesdays.
The High-Quality Rope he is currently shouting about is his prize inventory: Talon-Grip Cordage. It is woven from the inner bark of fire-resistant trees and reinforced with the hair of a mountain goat. Silas claims it is the only rope capable of gripping the "Slick-Stone" cliffs of the Rocky Hills. He acquired it through a dubious trade with a group of Goblins, whom he allegedly tricked by trading them a "bag of endless gold" that was actually just a bag of gold-painted river stones.
Silas possesses a surprisingly high Health of 60 due to his "Iron-Skin Tunic," a garment woven with metallic thread that he wears beneath his flamboyant silk robes. His fast-talking nature is a survival mechanism; he once successfully negotiated his way out of a Giant Spider's web by promising to return with a "tastier, fatter knight." He carries a "Coin-Seeker's Compass" that doesn't point North but instead twitches in the direction of the largest concentration of gold within a fifty-yard radius.
Despite his greed, Silas serves a vital role in the ecosystem of the wilds. He is a primary source of information, often trading rumors for a few gold pieces. He is particularly wary of the Dark Wizard, as Malakor’s presence tends to "kill the local economy," and he has been known to give small discounts to anyone who looks capable of inconveniencing the Obsidian Tower.
Pickpocket: 
The Pickpocket, often a member of the Slick-Hand Syndicate, is a master of "Low-Light Skulking," a technique that allows them to use the long shadows cast by the Crossroads' campfires to become nearly invisible. Their Evilness of 65reflects a cold, professional detachment; they don't just steal gold, but often take vital survival items—like a traveler's only water skin or a map—without regard for the victim's survival in the wilds.
They are equipped with Muffle-Boots, footwear made from the soft underbelly of a giant spider, which allows them to move across the gravel and dirt of the Crossroads in total silence. Their fingers are often coated in Grip-Oil, a substance that makes their touch undetectable against fabric while ensuring they never drop a lifted purse. Despite their low Health of 40, they are exceptionally difficult to corner because they carry Vanish-Dust—pulverized blue crystals from the caverns mixed with dried soot—which creates a blinding flash and a cloud of smoke when smashed.
A common tactic among these thieves is the "Cuckoo’s Gambit," where they steal an item from one traveler and hide it in the cart of a reputable merchant like Silas the Stout. They then retrieve the item later once the heat has died down. They are particularly wary of the Judgment-Sand at the Crossroads, which makes their footprints glow red; to counter this, many pickpockets walk on the wooden rails of stalls or jump between wagon roofs to avoid touching the ground.
Locals whisper that the Pickpockets are actually scouts for the Dark Wizard, tasked with stealing specific "memory-trinkets" that Malakor requires for his rituals at the Obsidian Tower. This would explain why they sometimes ignore bags of gold in favor of seemingly worthless family heirlooms or old letters.
Fisherman: 
Barnaby "The Still" is the sole permanent resident of the Misty Lake’s shoreline, an individual who has spent so many decades immersed in the Heavy Vapor that his skin has taken on a grey, translucent quality. Despite his Health of 55, he possesses an uncanny spiritual resilience to the lake’s memory-dampening effects. Barnaby does not fish for food; instead, he uses a rod made from a Phase-Vine and a hook of Cold-Iron to "dredge" the lake for Memory-Trinkets—physical manifestations of thoughts lost by travelers in the fog.
He wears a tattered coat woven from Drown-Lily fibers, which acts as a natural respirator, filtering the spectral mist before it can reach his lungs. Barnaby is the only person who possesses the Tide-Watcher’s Sight, a physiological adaptation that allows him to see the silhouette of the central island even when the fog is at its thickest. He is a silent guardian of the Navigator's legacy, believing that the Primal Compass must only be claimed by someone whose heart is "as still as the water."
His Evilness (0) is a reflection of his absolute neutrality. He rarely speaks because he believes that words are "leaks" that allow the lake to steal one's identity. When he does speak, his voice sounds like grinding silt. He keeps a small rowboat named The Anchorless, crafted from Ghost-Oak wood that produces no ripples or sound when moving through the water. It is rumored that Barnaby was once a sailor under the Great Navigator Aethelred and remained behind as a voluntary sentinel when the rest of the crew vanished into the Sunless Sea. He will occasionally trade "Lake-Glass"—smoothed shards of solidified memory—to travelers in exchange for stories of the world outside the mist, as he uses these stories to replace the memories the lake has slowly drained from him.

Waterspirit: 
The Water Spirit, known to the Fisherman as Nereia of the Veil, is a semi-sentient manifestation of the lake’s Heavy Vapor and the residual mana of the Sunless Sea. With an Evilness of 40, she is not a creature of malice, but of extreme territorial instinct; she views any ripple in the water or vibration in the air as a violent intrusion upon her mourning. Her form is a shifting, translucent silhouette that is nearly indistinguishable from the lake’s fog, except for two glowing, orbal eyes that reflect the blue light of the Crystal Cavern deep below.

Skeleton warior: 
The Skeleton Warriors, also known as the Marrow-Guards, are the reanimated remains of the ancient militia that once protected the valley during the Age of Ruin. Their bones have been stained a dark grey by centuries of exposure to the crypt’s Stagnation Aura, which has replaced their organic tissue with a brittle, magically-conductive mineral. With an Evilness of 100, they are entirely devoid of soul or memory, functioning as biological traps triggered by the presence of a living heartbeat. Their rusted armor is not just old equipment but has become part of their skeletal structure, fused together by the same necromantic energy that powers their movement.
Because they are driven by the Shadow-Debt of the crypt, they are particularly aggressive toward anyone carrying a light source other than the Ferryman’s Beacon, which they recognize as a symbol of authority. Despite having 65 health, they possess a trait called Relentless Assembly, allowing them to ignore the loss of minor limbs during combat. Their weapons are serrated Grave-Blades that have been known to cause a Chilled Wound, a magical injury that refuses to heal until the victim returns to the warmth of the Starting Village. Rumors among the village elders suggest that each skeleton is bound to a specific Lineage Rune on the crypt walls, and destroying the corresponding rune is the only way to ensure the warrior never rises again. They are often found standing perfectly still in the Silence Chambers, appearing as nothing more than decorative piles of bone until a traveler breaks the silence.

Ghost: 
The Ghost, known as a Sorrow-Wraith, is an Echo-Shade that has gained sufficient density to interact with the physical world. Unlike the mindless Skeleton Warriors, these phantoms retain fragmented consciousness consisting entirely of their most traumatic memories. Their Evilness of 80 is driven by a state of eternal mourning and a parasitic need to drain heat from the living to sustain their ethereal form.
The phantom's freezing touch is a localized manifestation of the crypt’s Stagnation Aura; when a Ghost passes through a living being, it temporarily halts the molecular motion of the victim’s blood, causing a condition known as Frost-Shock. Their wailing is a sonic weapon that mimics the Crying Gale of the Rocky Hills, designed to shatter the focus of intruders and draw more Echo-Shades to their location.
Because they are phased into the ethereal plane, they possess a trait called Intangible Drift, allowing them to bypass the Silence Chambers' walls and the Weight-Traps of the lower levels. Despite their 45 Health, they are highly resistant to non-magical physical attacks, though they react violently to the blue light of the Ferryman’s Beacon, which causes their spectral essence to evaporate.
Legends state that these phantoms are the spirits of those who died during the Great Stasis ritual performed by Elder Valerius, forever trapped in the moment of their transition because they were never properly recorded in the Ever-Ink Ledger. They are often found hovering near the Lineage Runes of families that have no living survivors, searching for a name they no longer remember.

CrystalGolem: 
The Crystal Golem, known as the Resonant Warden, is a massive artificial construct serving as the physical immune system of the Crystal Cavern. It is composed of geologically bonded granite and high-density Azure Resonators, making it a living extension of the cavern’s own energy grid. With a Health of 150, it is one of the most durable entities in the realm, possessing a carapace that is nearly impervious to standard steel. Its low Evilness of 10 indicates that it lacks individual malice or a desire to hunt; it is strictly a programmed sentinel designed to maintain the sanctity of the Monolith of Echoes. The Golem remains in a state of suspended animation, appearing as a jagged rock formation, until the specific frequency of the blue crystals is disrupted by the removal of a gem or an act of violence. Once activated, the Golem utilizes a trait called Harmonic Resonance, where every movement it makes generates a low-frequency hum that can shatter glass and disorient the equilibrium of living beings. Its primary method of attack involves discharging stored mana through its crystalline fists, creating a Prismatic Pulse that deals both physical and magical damage. Because it is powered by the ambient energy of the cavern, the Golem is virtually tireless and can regenerate minor structural damage as long as it remains in contact with the cavern floor. Its only known weakness is the Absolute Silence of the room; if a traveler can magically dampen the Golem's internal vibrations, its limbs become heavy and its reaction time slows significantly. Legend suggests the Golem was built using the soul-fragments of the first oracles, who chose to remain as eternal protectors of the deep earth’s secrets.
